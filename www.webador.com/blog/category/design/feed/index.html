How to safely use images on your website

Are you using an image that isn’t yours? Then it’s crucial to check whether you have permission. Simply grabbing images from Google can get you into serious trouble, as you don’t own the photo.
Therefore, we offer two options for your search for the perfect images:

Option 1: Use Images from Google

If you find an image on Google that perfectly suits your website, you can’t directly post it on your website. What you can do is track down the owner. Once you’ve found the owner, you can request written permission. This way, you can post the image on your website without worry.
However, using Google Images remains risky, as you can never be certain whether a copyrighted image has been reuploaded elsewhere.

Option 2: Use stock photo websites

The safest and fastest way to find images is through stock photo websites. Here you can choose from hundreds of thousands of photos that you can use on your website for free.

]]> You probably recognize this: you spend hours perfecting your product text, shooting stunning product photos, and fine-tuning your logo. But as soon as it comes to the “About Me” page, or that photo of yourself on the homepage, you might hesitate. “Doesn’t my product speak for itself?” or “Do people really care about my face?”. But did you know that customers actually want to buy from you?

Nothing could be further from the truth. Especially as a starting entrepreneur, you are your most important asset. In this blog post, we’ll explain why hiding behind your logo is a waste of revenue, and how you can effortlessly create that personal connection.

Tell your own story

Let’s face it: chances are there are others selling or doing roughly the same thing as you. In a world overflowing with options, the product is often the reason people search, but you’re the reason they stay and make a purchase.

When a potential customer sees the driving force behind your company, a human connection is instantly created. People don’t buy from a business registration number; they buy from someone they feel comfortable with. Your passion, your smile, and even your unique personality create a trust factor that a large, anonymous organization can never match. Creating a strong corporate identity that truly reflects your personality is incredibly helpful.

Why a face on your website immediately leads to more conversions

It’s even scientifically proven: our brains are programmed to recognize faces incredibly quickly. In a fraction of a second, we scan a photo to determine whether we can trust someone. This is what we call “social proof.”

  • The first 50 milliseconds: Research shows that visitors form an opinion about your website in the blink of an eye. A human face ensures that first impression is immediately positive and “safe.”
  • Oxytocin rush: When we see a smiling face, our brains release a small amount of oxytocin, also known as the “cuddle hormone” or “trust hormone.” This lowers the barrier for your visitor to contact you.
  • Recognition: We remember images much better than dry text. By showing yourself, you ensure that your brand is not just a name, but a person who stays with us.

A website without a personal face can quickly feel cold and distant. By showing yourself, you lower the barrier to contact. A customer no longer thinks, “I’ll send an email to a company,” but rather, “I’ll send a quick message to [Your Name].” This lowered barrier to entry immediately leads to more inquiries, more sales, and loyal customers who feel connected to you.

You don’t need an expensive photographer!

Many entrepreneurs put off “being visible” because they dread an expensive, posed photoshoot. But there’s no need! Those spontaneous, authentic photos often perform better than those stiff studio shots.

Here are a few tips to help you take a great photo today:

  • Get help: Ask a friend, family member, or partner to take photos of you for an hour. This creates a relaxed atmosphere, and that will definitely show in your photos.
  • Seek out the light: Stand outside or near a large window. Natural light is, of course, the best filter available.
  • Keep it simple: A simple background will keep the focus on you.
  • Smile like you really do: Think of something that makes you happy, or have the person taking the photo tell a joke. A genuine smile exudes confidence.

Are you happy with your photos? Then don’t forget to upload them correctly. It’s important to optimize your images for Google, so your website is not only personalized but also easier to find.

Your product is the solution you offer, but you are the reason the customer chooses that solution. Dare to show yourself. It doesn’t have to be perfect; it should be genuine.

My challenge to you: Replace one generic image on your website or social media with a photo of yourself today. You’ll see the positive impact this will have on your website!

]]> So you want to start a website? That’s great news! There’s always room for a new, creative voice on the internet. But what should you start with? In this article, we share 8 ideas for your own website, each with a lot of potential. Once you’ve chosen an idea, it’s important to put your own spin on it to stand out, because that’s how you become unique.

Ready for the first idea?

1: Blog

The first idea for creating your own website is to start a blog . What could be more fun than writing about topics you find interesting?

Then you might be wondering: isn’t a blog a bit old-fashioned? 

No. Absolutely not. As long as people use the internet to search for information, a blog isn’t outdated. Your blog posts can appear in search results, helping people find your website. 

Whether it’s about pets or financial tips… the topics people are searching for are topics you can be found for. And that creates opportunities!

Over time, as you generate significant traffic to your website, you can even link your blog to various revenue models. Think of selling courses, placing affiliate links, or promoting specific products.

 

2: Webshop

By starting an online store , you can sell products you’re passionate about.

“But what on earth can I sell? ”

Consider the following questions:

  • Which products do you like to use?
  • What gets you excited?
  • And which products are difficult to obtain? 

Perhaps these are products that you can sell with your webshop.

A portfolio website allows you to showcase your work to the world. This makes a portfolio website a powerful tool for acquiring new clients. Such a website offers opportunities, particularly for:

A portfolio website allows you to showcase your creative work, giving people a good idea of ​​what you could do for them.

This idea is very similar to a portfolio website. The main difference between these two websites is that the portfolio website focuses more on showcasing creative work, while the freelance website focuses more on promoting freelance work.

This website features affiliate links, allowing you to generate income by recommending products. 

An affiliate link is a link that tells the seller which affiliate placed an order. This affiliate then receives a commission. This is a significant source of income, especially for bloggers.

The reason that online stores like Amazon.com became the go-to place for online shopping was partly due to the launch of Amazon Affiliates. This gave websites around the world the opportunity to direct their web traffic to Amazon in exchange for a commission after the sale. 

By writing reviews or blog posts you can drive traffic to your affiliate website, where you can then place affiliate links.

The final website idea we’d like to share is an educational website. If you’re adept in a particular field, you can package and sell your knowledge on your educational website. You can sell various courses on this website, or create a portal where you publish monthly content.

This educational website allows people to acquire your knowledge for a fee. However, it’s important to specialize in one area of ​​expertise with this website so you can build authority more quickly.

Conclusion

In this article, we’ve shared 8 ideas for creating your own website. It’s important to give these ideas a personal touch so your new website stands out more quickly. There’s always room on the internet for a new creative voice.

]]> When building a website or online store, you might primarily think about images, colors, and products. Yet, something you don’t immediately see plays an equally important role: the fonts you use. A good font combination ensures that your website looks professional, is easy to read, and has a beautiful appearance. Crucial to your online success!

In this blog post, we’ll guide you step-by-step through choosing and combining fonts. We’ll provide practical tips and examples that you can easily apply yourself. This way, you can get started right away and ensure your texts look their best.

1. Types of Fonts

Before you start combining fonts, it’s helpful to know what types of fonts there are, and what each type is used for.

  • Serif: These are fonts with small “feet” or strokes at the end of the letters. They are often used in classic, elegant, and formal settings. This font is good for titles, but less ideal for longer texts.
  • Sans-serif: A typeface that is often uncluttered and easier to read on a screen. This is ideal for longer texts on websites.
  • Decorative fonts: Think of display fonts, handwritten styles, ornate lettering, etc. These are great for visual elements or as headings on your website. Just make sure they remain legible.

Tip: Keep it simple. Choose one primary font for all your text and consider one eye-catching font for your logo and headlines.

2. The importance of font combinations

You might be wondering if using multiple fonts is actually necessary. The answer is: not necessarily, but it can give your website a lot more power.

A good font combination helps you with:

  1. Recognition and atmosphere: say you sell handmade jewelry — then a sleek display font for your headlines works well, combined with a clean sans-serif for your product descriptions.
  2. Readability and structure: different styles between headings and your general text make it easier for your visitor to scan your page
  3. Professionalism: An incorrectly chosen font can make visitors doubt the quality of your website.

3. What should you pay attention to when combining fonts?

There are 4 things to keep in mind when combining your fonts:

  1. Character & Appearance:
    Ask yourself: what kind of feeling do I want to evoke? Playful, reliable, luxurious, warm? Make sure your fonts reflect that.
  2. Structure & Hierarchy:
    Ensure a clear distinction in size or style between your headings and body text. This helps your visitors quickly see what’s important.
  3. Contrast & Harmony:
    A good combination should have enough difference to differentiate, but also enough similarities to be cohesive.
  4. Stuck? Keep it simple!
    At a loss? Then choose a single font with multiple styles (light, regular, Italian, bold). This creates a sense of calm and prevents mismatches.
    For example: use “Poppins Regular” for your body text and “Poppins Bold” for your headings.

4. Common mistakes

Be aware of these pitfalls when combining fonts:

  • Too many fonts at once: Do you have three or more different fonts on a page? That can quickly become chaotic! Stick to a maximum of two fonts.
  • Decorative font as body text : No matter how beautiful a decorative font is, if your font is difficult to read, visitors will quickly drop out.
  • No attention to mobile: Always test whether your fonts are also easy to read on the mobile version of your website.
  • Lack of consistency: Use the same font combination on every page. This creates cohesion and recognizability.

A well-chosen font combination may be subtle, but it can have a big impact. It increases readability, creates recognition, and creates a professional look.

]]> As a starting entrepreneur, you may not be a web designer, and that’s perfectly fine! But did you know that good web design is essential for the success of your online presence? A well-designed website not only attracts more visitors, but also converts them into customers.

In this blog, we dive into the world of web design and give you 5 practical tips to take your website to the next level.

Let’s start with a brief explanation of what web design actually entails. Simply put, web design is the process of designing your website or webshop. Think about the colors, fonts, page layout and overall look and feel. It’s your digital business card!

So start with these 5 web design tips for beginners!

Tip 1: Simplicity is the strength of good design

When you start designing a website, you can quickly become overwhelmed by all the elements you can put on your website. You may want to put everything on your website, from pop-ups to complex implementations. We advise against this at all times! This is a common mistake. A clear website is much more effective.

For each new element you want to add, ask yourself the following questions:

  • What is my ideal visitor looking for?
  • What is my visitor’s goal on this page?
  • Does the element help my visitor achieve their goal?
  • Does this element add value to the page?

If you answered ‘no’ to the last two questions, it might be better not to include the element on your website. A complicated website is not good for conversion and user-friendliness. Conversion is the moment when a visitor takes action, such as making a purchase or contacting you. For a successful website, keep your design simple and clear.

Tip 2: Don’t use more than 3 fonts

With so many fonts available, it’s tempting to go wild. But one of the golden rules of web design is: don’t use more than three different fonts on your entire website. Each font has its own personality, so choose one that fits your company and corporate identity.

For example:

  • Do you have a business? Then go for a sleek and simple font.
  • Do you have a beauty salon? An elegant font for your header would be a good fit.

One of the golden rules of web design is that when designing a website, it is important to use the same fonts on every page. Not only should the fonts be consistent, but also their size, thickness, and style. This will give your entire website the same visual identity.

Once you have chosen a font, it is important to check its readability: not only on your desktop, but also on your mobile phone. Nowadays, the majority of your visitors will be coming from mobile devices, so it is crucial to optimize your web design for this.

To maintain consistency in your web design, it is important to note which fonts you use for which purpose (for example: ‘Title: Font A’, ‘General text: Font B’).

Tip : Search Google for “font combinations” for inspiration!

Tip 3: Use a color palette

Of course, you want people to recognize your website. A large part of that recognition comes from the colors used in your website. Colors give meaning to your brand and help people remember it. Think of Facebook (blue), Coca-Cola (red), McDonald’s (yellow).

To create that recognizability, choose a color palette of no more than three different colors for your website. This makes your website easy on the eye and easier to remember.

When choosing colors, it is especially important to determine what you want the website to convey. Colors have a psychological effect:

  • Blue: calm & tranquility, fresh, protective, peaceful, technical, formal, businesslike, wisdom, stability, hope, reliability, infinity, cold, truth, clarity, power
  • Red: action, dynamism, vitality, liveliness, romance, love, warmth, energy, power, danger, commanding, aggressive
  • Green: nature, relaxation, leisure, calm, peace.
  • Orange: joy, inspiring, tasty, interesting, active, warmth, stimulation, warning
  • Yellow: warming, light, stimulation, clarity, cheerful, playfulness, curiosity, energy
  • Purple: melancholy, luxury, intelligence, magic, vanity, artistic, spirituality, refinement
  • Brown: earthy, stylish, completeness, stability, importance
  • Black: authority, power, timeless, elegance, death, rebellion
  • Gray: emotionless, boring, meaningless, old age
  • White: innocence, luxury, simplicity, light, pure, neutral, timeless

Choose the colors that match your brand story and the emotion you want to convey!

Tip 4: Use images that speak for themselves

The saying “a picture is worth a thousand words” certainly applies to your website. The right images can set the tone and reinforce your message.

Make sure the images on your website fit your business and convey the right feeling to your visitors. The best images are your own photos; this gives a personal and authentic touch. Don’t have your own photos (yet)? No problem! Websites such as Unsplash offer an extensive library of beautiful, free, high-quality images.

Tip 5: Test how your website looks on mobile phones

We cannot stress this enough: the majority of your website visitors access your site via their mobile phones. This means that your web design must look perfect on a small screen.

In the world of web design, this is called responsive design. It means that your web design always looks good, regardless of the device used to view the website. At Webador, all designs are responsive. This saves you a lot of time! However, it is always a good idea to check your website to make sure that your page is displayed correctly on all devices.

]]> Launching a small business website can feel overwhelming with all the options available. Should you add a blog? Showcase customer reviews? Include a photo gallery?

While these features can add value, there are five essential pages every small business website needs to build trust, attract customers, and establish a strong online presence.

Let’s look into the five must-have pages and the role each one plays.

Homepage – Your digital storefront

When a customer visits your page, your homepage is usually the first thing they see. This means you really want your homepage to stand out so they stick around.

It needs to quickly explain what your business does, who it helps, and why people should care. A good homepage includes a short description of your main product or service, a professional photo or image, and a clear call-to-action like “Get a Quote” or “Shop Now.” Keep the layout simple and make sure your navigation is easy to follow. You can also add your contact details here, and they should be easy to find.


Think of your homepage as your first impression, or a business card. It should give people a reason to stay and explore further.

About Page – Build trust by sharing your story

The About page is where customers go to learn who you are. This is your chance to share your business story and show why you’re the right person or team for the job.
Explain how your business started, what motivates you, and what makes you different. Include a photo of yourself or your team if possible. People connect with people, and showing the real
faces behind the business helps build trust.
You don’t need to go into too much detail. Just enough to give visitors confidence that they’re working or buying from someone who cares.

Services or Products Page – Show what you offer

Here you can explain what your business provides. Be as clear and specific as possible.
Whether you’re selling physical products, offering services, or doing both, this page should help
visitors understand exactly how you can help them.


List your main offerings, include short descriptions, and if possible, show prices or starting rates.
Use images where relevant and keep the layout easy to scan. If you offer several distinct
services, consider giving each one its own page.


Make sure it’s easy for customers to take action, whether that’s making a purchase, booking a
service, or getting in touch.

Tip: Use product categories if you have a lot of products in your online store to make navigation easier.

Contact Page – Make it easy for your customers to reach you.

It should never be hard for someone to contact you, especially a potential customer. A good Contact page removes any barriers and gives visitors several ways to get in touch.


So what should be included on your page? Include your phone number, email address, and business address if you have one. Add a contact form for convenience, especially for mobile users. You can also include business hours, a map, and links to your social media pages.


Let people know how quickly you typically respond. This small detail helps manage expectations
and shows that you value communication.

Terms and Conditions – Set clear Rules

Your Terms and Conditions page covers the legal details that protect both your business and
your customers. It explains how you handle things like refunds, cancellations, deliveries, and
use of your website or services.


Even if you don’t sell online, this page can be useful for setting expectations around your
services or how customer data is handled. If you collect personal information, you should also
include a Privacy Policy, either on this page or as a separate one.


Having clear, accessible policies helps build trust and makes your business look more
professional.

Conclusion

Once these five pages are live, your website will be ready to support your business and grow
with it. Over time, you can add more features like a blog, reviews, or photo galleries. But these
five pages are the core that every small business needs.

At this time of year, you may want to offer your customers an extra unique experience. Maybe you’re already offering a holiday discount, or communicating longer delivery times on your website.

Changing your website (or store page) banner can immediately give your online store a whole different look. Let your creativity flow – in Canva, for example – and make a festive banner for the holidays.

Postal services are much busier during the holiday season, so you may be dealing with longer delivery times. Perhaps you also have a cut-off date for orders to be shipped in time for Christmas.

To keep your customers informed and help ensure gifts are placed under the tree in time, you can enable the top bar on your website. This way you can clearly communicate delivery times, a cut-off date, and more.
