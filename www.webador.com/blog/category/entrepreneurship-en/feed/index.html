]]> Artificial Intelligence has become an essential tool for website owners. Whether you are using ChatGPT to craft the perfect “About Us” page or Midjourney to generate a stunning header image, AI tools save time and money.

However, as of August 2, 2026, new transparency rules have officially taken effect under the EU AI Act (the European Union’s Artificial Intelligence Act).

Does this mean you now have to put a warning label on every single sentence written by ChatGPT? The short answer is: No! So what should you actually look out for?

At Webador, we have summarized exactly what the new AI labeling requirements mean for your website and how you can easily stay compliant.

The Biggest Myth: “All AI Content Must Be Labeled”

Let’s clear up a widespread misconception right away: There is no blanket labeling requirement for all AI-generated content.

The core goal of the EU AI Act is to prevent users from being deceived online. Visitors should be able to tell whether they are interacting with a human or a machine, especially when it comes to sensitive topics. Therefore, the rules depend entirely on your specific use case.

What Actually Needs an AI Label (And What Doesn’t)

As a website owner, blogger, or small business, there are three main categories of AI content you need to monitor:

1. Images, Video, and Audio

If you publish images, videos, or audio content that has been entirely generated or significantly altered by AI (often referred to as “deepfakes”), you must clearly label them.

  • Example: A photorealistic image in your blog post that you generated using Midjourney, DALL-E, or Stable Diffusion.
  • Exception: If you only used AI tools to adjust the brightness of a real photograph, remove a background, or retouch minor blemishes, you do not need to label it.

2. Texts and Blog Posts

This is where most website owners can breathe a sigh of relief. According to the EU guidelines, AI-generated text generally only needs a label if two conditions are met simultaneously:

  1. The text covers matters of public interest (e.g., hard news, politics, societal issues, medical advice).
  2. The text was published without significant human oversight (editorial review).

The good news: For everyday e-commerce product descriptions, standard marketing copy, translations, or grammar corrections, this labeling obligation usually does not apply.

Pro Tip – Always review AI content before it goes live. If you take a draft from ChatGPT, fact-check it, edit it, and adapt it to your own brand voice. This way you are taking editorial responsibility. In most cases, this removes the need for an AI label!

3. AI Chatbots and Customer Service

Have you integrated an automated AI chatbot on your website to handle customer support? If so, you must inform your visitors that they are chatting with a machine—unless it is very obvious from the context.

What About Webador’s Built-In Features and Image Gallery?

You might be wondering about the content you get directly through our platform. Here is how the EU AI Act applies to Webador’s specific features:

The Webador Image Gallery:

The free stock images available in our built-in gallery are standard, royalty-free photographs. You do not need to add an AI label to these images, as they are real stock photos, not AI-generated deepfakes.

Webador AI Text Generation:

If you use Webador’s built-in AI features to draft text for your website, the same “Text” rules mentioned above apply. Since you are using it to build typical website pages (like your homepage or services page) and reviewing the text before hitting publish, you take on editorial responsibility and generally do not need to label it.

How to Label AI Content Correctly in Practice

The EU does not demand complex technical watermarks from everyday website owners. The law simply requires clear and understandable information. Here is how you can easily implement this on your site:

  • For Images: Add a short note directly in the image caption or alt text (e.g., “Image source: AI-generated via Midjourney”).
  • For Texts (if required): Place a simple sentence at the bottom of the article (e.g., “This article was drafted with the assistance of Artificial Intelligence.”).
  • General Transparency: Adding a brief transparency statement to your Imprint (Legal Notice) or Privacy Policy page is always a smart, future-proof idea.

What Are the Penalties for Non-Compliance?

While the EU AI Act theoretically includes massive fines, the real danger for small website owners and freelancers comes from a different direction: warnings from competitors. If you secretly use AI-generated content to gain an unfair competitive advantage, you make yourself vulnerable to legal cease-and-desist letters. A quick transparency note saves you a lot of legal headaches.

Conclusion: Keep Building Your Website Without Panic

You can absolutely continue using AI tools to make your Webador website look and sound professional. As long as you use AI as a creative assistant to shape your ideas and manually review the results, you are on the safe side. It’s important to add a transparent label for completely AI-generated photos, automated chatbots, or unedited news articles.

The Benefits of Building Your Own Website

1.You’ll save money

Building a website can easily cost hundreds to thousands of euros—especially if you want to update it frequently. By building your own website, you can save that budget for other investments, such as marketing, photography, or purchasing.

2. You can make changes right away

What should you keep in mind when building a website yourself?

Building a website yourself is easy, but a good website does require a structured foundation. Consider the following:

  • Choosing a clear domain name: A short and memorable domain name makes it easier for customers to remember your website.
  • A professional look: Use no more than 3 colors, clear fonts, and high-quality images.
  • Write from the customer’s perspective: Don’t just explain what you do, focus on how you help your customers. Answer frequently asked questions on your website and clearly explain why someone should choose your company.

When is outsourcing a good choice?

Although many aspiring entrepreneurs are perfectly capable of building their own websites, there are also situations in which it makes sense to have a website built for you. This is the case, for example, when you need a complex website with specific features. Think of advanced animations, a customer portal, or integrations with external software that aren’t available as standard.

What should you keep in mind when having a website built?

It’s important to think ahead about what having a website built will mean for you in the long run. For many changes, you’ll be dependent on the company that built your website. In addition, a custom-built website is less flexible than you might think. You initially pay for the design and development, but costs may arise afterward for maintenance, technical updates, or new features.

For most aspiring entrepreneurs, it’s therefore not necessary to hire a professional in the early stages. A website you can manage yourself often offers everything you need to successfully start a business.

As your business grows, you can always expand your website further or choose to hire a professional!

Leah launched Leah O’Grady Art in 2020, transforming her lifelong love of drawing into a brand that celebrates Ireland’s cultural heritage. Her work focuses on two beloved icons: nostalgic Irish pub fronts and vintage postal stamps. With over 60 pubs illustrated so far from the famous city institutions to the small, rural gems Leah is determined to give each one its moment to shine.

“Many of these pubs have sadly been closing after generations of serving their communities,” Leah shares. “Through my art, I hope to capture their spirit, evoke memories, and celebrate the role they’ve played in Irish life.”

From Hobby to Business

Creativity had always been part of Leah’s life. Alongside her work as a Special Needs Assistant in a primary school, she enjoyed arts and crafts both in the classroom and at home. But it wasn’t until 2020 that she began creating pieces more seriously.

At first, her illustrations were just for herself and her family. But when she shared them online, the response was overwhelming. People not only loved her nostalgic designs – they wanted to buy them. That encouragement was the turning point: her passion project became a small business.

Sharing a Piece of Ireland

The heart of Leah’s work lies in memory, connection, and identity. Her illustrations of cosy pub fronts and vintage stamps aren’t just decorative, they carry with them stories of belonging.

“Many young people have left Ireland to live abroad, and I think there’s a deep grá – a love and longing – for home that never really leaves them,”

That’s why her website has been essential. Acting as both a portfolio and shopfront, it allows customers to explore her full range and purchase easily from anywhere in the world.

Often at markets, someone might love a piece but prefer to take a card and browse online later. My website makes that possible.”

It also streamlines the business side of things such as automating order confirmations and shipping notifications, while offering interactive features like polls where customers can suggest the next pub to be illustrated.

Overcoming Challenges

Like many artists, Leah has faced moments of self-doubt. Pricing her work was another early challenge, balancing the hours of design, production, and market days with customer expectations.

Her solution? Trusting her creative vision and connecting with other small business owners for support.

“Networking has been incredibly helpful,” she explains. “Talking to other like-minded artists allows you to share ideas, get advice, and gain confidence to keep going.”

Memorable Milestones

Her mission is clear: to continue expanding her collection of Irish pubs and vintage stamps, celebrating the places and symbols that hold a special place in people’s hearts.

As Leah puts it:

 “My goal is to make it easy for people to bring a little piece of Ireland into their daily lives, no matter where they are.”

Research into consumer behavior shows that customers are quite willing to wait, provided the communication is clear beforehand. After all, transparency builds trust!

With this holiday checklist, you ensure that everything is well organized before you leave.

1. Manage your customers’ expectations

Ignorance is the biggest conversion killer! Therefore, you need to be transparent with your website visitors:

  • The Top Bar : In the Webador editor, you can easily place a top bar at the top of your website.
    A short message on your website can prevent many questions. Let visitors know that you are on vacation and what this means for orders or inquiries.
  • Update your contact page: Mention on this page that response times are longer than usual. Optionally, you can create an FAQ page and direct visitors there so they can find the most frequently asked questions themselves!

2. Enable an automatic email response

An automatic out-of-office message is a simple way to manage expectations. As soon as someone sends you an email, they immediately receive a reply stating that you are on vacation and when you can respond again.

This way, customers feel heard, even if you cannot answer immediately.

Here is how to set up an out-of-office message for:
  1. Your Webmail
  2. Outlook
  3. Gmail

3. Check your webshop stock

Do you have a webshop? Then there are two options possible as soon as you go on vacation:

  • Option A : You keep your webshop open but communicate everywhere that delivery times are longer than usual. Don’t forget to mention when you will be back and when you can ship the order!
  • Option B : You temporarily disable the shopping cart in the settings. Customers will then not be able to order anything from your webshop, as it will be (temporarily) offline.

4. Plan your content in advance

Even when you are on vacation, your online visibility doesn’t have to come to a complete standstill. By scheduling some content for your socials in advance, your company remains visible without you having to work on it daily. According to marketing experts, consistency is more important than quantity.

  • Coosto
  • Canva
  • Later

5. The Webador App

The urge to quickly check if an order has come in or if an email has arrived often remains present among starting entrepreneurs. That is where the Webador mobile app comes in handy! Ideal for small, urgent matters that cannot wait until you are at home.

Although the app is super handy, there is also a danger lurking: the temptation to peek too often. That is why we would like to give you a tip: set a check time with yourself. For example, 10 minutes in the morning, or that you are only allowed to check on Sundays. Above all, keep in mind that you are on vacation to relax!

Go on vacation well prepared

Taking a break from your business is not only nice, but also important. With a few small preparations, you ensure that your website continues to work while you enjoy your vacation.

So just go through this checklist before you leave. That way, you can relax and come back full of new ideas.

  1. VAT Return (When can depend on the country you are from)
  2. Income tax (once a year)

Add these deadlines to your calendar immediately and enable an automatic reminder. That way, you won’t have to keep thinking about it in between, and you’ll be notified on time.

Tip: even if you haven’t had any revenue, you often still have to file a tax return!

#2: Keep your records organized

Administration sounds bigger than it is; it doesn’t have to be difficult as long as you keep up with it. As accounting expert Marjan Heemskerk says: “You keep books primarily for yourself, not just for the Tax Authorities.” A few simple habits can make a world of difference:

  • Use a separate business bank account .
  • Keep your receipts and invoices in digital format .
  • Update your accounts regularly (weekly or monthly).

Don’t view your administration as something for later, but as a tool for yourself. The better you have an overview, the less stress you will experience regarding your tax return.

#3 Use Aids

There are plenty of tools that can help you with the subject of tax; you certainly don’t have to do it all yourself! Consider:

  • Accounting software: It centralizes your income/expenses and calculates VAT automatically.
  • Calendar alerts: Use dedicated apps or simple reminders to never miss a deadline.
  • Webador Export: If you have an online store with us, you can export your orders for your accounting with a single click. No more manual searching!

Do you have a webshop with Webador? Then you’ve come to the right place! You can easily export your orders for your administration. This way, you don’t have to search for everything individually, and you’ll be finished faster.

#4 Prepare your tax return

Don’t wait until the day before the deadline. Prepare the ground in advance:

  • Gather your data: Revenue, business expenses, supporting documents.
  • Check your deductions: Find out about allowances for self-employed individuals or new entrepreneurs.
  • Request an extension: If you cannot meet the deadline, request a postponement in time.

#5 Perfection isn’t necessary, but being on time is!

One of the biggest pitfalls for starting entrepreneurs is the idea that everything has to be perfect right away, but that is not necessary at all! The Tax Authorities do not expect a perfect entrepreneur, but an honest entrepreneur who files their return on time.

It is far more important that you file your return with the information you have at that moment than that you keep delaying because you are still unsure about a detail. Are you unsure about something? You can often adjust it or ask about it later. Correcting a mistake is usually not a problem; being late, however, can cause unnecessary stress.

Conclusion

With a little preparation, established habits, and the right tools, doing your taxes will no longer stress you out. And the less time you spend on peripheral matters, the more room you have left for what you really enjoy: growing your business.

Don’t worry, it doesn’t have to be complicated. In this blog, we’ll tell you the most important rules regarding privacy, cookies, your terms and conditions, and more! Make sure your website is legally compliant!

1: The Privacy Statement

Do you collect personal data via your website? For example, through a contact form, newsletter subscription, or webshop? Then, according to privacy legislation (the General Data Protection Regulation, or GDPR), you are required to post a privacy statement on your website. This statement informs your customers and visitors what personal data you collect, what you do with it, and why.

Your privacy statement must include the following:

  • What data you collect (such as name, email address, postal address)
  • Why you collect that data
  • How long you store it
  • Whether you share the data with others (for example, for payments to external parties)

Tip: Use the privacy statement from termly.io. They will help you with a basic text for your own privacy statement. Then create a new page via Webador and name it ‘privacy statement’. Add the generated text to the page and place the page in the footer of your website.

2: Cookies

At Webador, you don’t have to worry much about the cookies that are automatically placed on your website. We have taken measures to ensure that these cookies comply with privacy legislation:

  1. We use Google Analytics to gain insight into the use of your website. To guarantee the privacy of your website visitors, we have set the cookies so that IP addresses are anonymized and Google is not allowed to use the data for other services.
  2. We place functional cookies. We place a functional cookie that is necessary to check whether you are the owner of your website. This cookie has no further influence on the privacy of your visitors.

For these standard cookies, it is not mandatory to add a cookie notification to your website. Only if you add additional external elements, such as a YouTube video, do we recommend placing a cookie notification! You can easily activate this via your website settings.

3: General Terms and Conditions

Want to avoid confusion? Include information in your terms and conditions about how you handle warranties, cancellations, who is responsible, and how payment is made. That way, your customers, and visitors know exactly where they stand.

The terms and conditions are the rules that apply to an agreement, for example, when a customer purchases a product or service from you.

Good to know: terms and conditions are not mandatory, but they can offer you a lot of clarity and certainty!

Drawing up your general terms and conditions

  • You can find examples of general terms and conditions online, or ask ChatGPT to draw up a document for you!
  • Some industries have standard terms and conditions that you must add.

Topics that frequently appear in the general terms and conditions:

  • Quotation (is it non-binding or binding, and what is the acceptance period)
  • Payment
  • Delivery
  • Transport (who pays for transport and what are the shipping costs)
  • Warranty (if applicable, under what conditions)
  • Liability
  • Cooling-off period (i.e., right of withdrawal)
Read here for more information on terms and conditions for your webshop

4: Other

There are a number of legal issues that may be useful for your site, but this depends on what exactly you post on your website:

  • A Disclaimer: This allows you to limit certain liabilities, for example for the content of external links or the completeness and accuracy of the information on your website. A disclaimer can therefore add some nuance (you remain responsible for the basics, of course).
  • An ‘Intellectual Property’ notice: Have you written some great texts, taken beautiful photos, or designed a recognizable logo? Then it’s a good idea to let people know that these things are yours and are protected by copyright, for example. This prevents others from simply taking them and using them without permission.
  • Codes of conduct: Are you a member of a trade association with certain rules? Then it is good to mention this. This applies, for example, to travel agencies, real estate agents, lawyers, and construction companies.
  • The small print for promotions: Do you have a great promotion or special offer? Make sure the terms and conditions are clear. What exactly does the promotion entail? How long does it last? Are there any exceptions? Describe this in a way that is immediately clear to everyone, so that you and others can always refer back to it without any problems.

Conclusion: a legally well-regulated website helps you gain trust.

Investing in this legal foundation ensures that your website meets all requirements and gives your visitors a sense of security. It shows that you take rules and personal data seriously. Don’t wait any longer and check now whether the legal aspects of your website are up-to-date!
